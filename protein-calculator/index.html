How this is calculated (and the research behind it)

Resting metabolism (BMR). The calculator uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, the predictive equation validated as most accurate for healthy adults.1 For women it is 10 × weight(kg) + 6.25 × height(cm) − 5 × age − 161; for men the last term is + 5.

Total burn (TDEE). BMR is multiplied by a physical-activity factor (1.2 sedentary to 1.9 very active), the standard way to scale resting metabolism to real-world expenditure.2 Your calorie target is TDEE minus the deficit implied by your chosen pace.

Weight-loss trajectory. The pace-to-deficit conversion uses the classic estimate that about 7,700 kcal equals one kilogram of body weight (3,500 kcal per pound).3 This is a first approximation. Real loss slows over time as metabolism adapts, so a dynamic model predicts less than a straight line at the far end.4 Treat the timeline as a planning estimate, not a promise.

Protein target. The 1.6 to 2.2 g/kg range comes from lean-mass research, where benefit to fat-free mass leveled off near 1.6 g/kg.5 A GLP-1 advisory from four medical societies uses a lower range, 1.2 to 1.6 g/kg, which the calculator shows beside it. People with kidney disease should ask a clinician before raising protein. The target is split across your chosen number of meals because distribution matters more than any single dose.6

Macros and fiber. The macro row sets protein at 1.6 g/kg, fat at 25 percent of the calorie target (inside the 20 to 35 percent range the National Academies give for adults), and carbohydrate as what remains.9 Fiber is 14 g per 1,000 calories, the Dietary Guidelines figure.10 Fiber matters on a GLP-1 because constipation is one of the most common side effects. Raise it gradually and with fluids, since a sudden jump can worsen bloating.

Meal timing on a small appetite. The same distribution logic is why the meal planner lets you spread the target across three to five meals: dividing protein into moderate servings through the day supports muscle better than concentrating it in one or two large meals, which is hard to eat anyway when a GLP-1 has cut your hunger.6 On low-appetite days, eat protein first, front-load it earlier when hunger is highest, use liquid sources like a shake or Greek yogurt, and keep the biggest meal away from the peak side-effect window after a dose.

The two muscle lines. Reviews of the trial data put lean mass at roughly 15 to 40 percent of the weight lost on a GLP-1.7 The dashed red line models the high end, 40 percent, that occurs without countermeasures. The green band models the outcome with adequate protein and resistance training. Its lower edge, about 2 percent, applies the finding that resistance training offset about 93.5 percent of the lean mass that caloric restriction alone would cost8; that result comes from older adults with obesity who were not taking a GLP-1, so it is an extrapolation. Its upper edge, 15 percent, is the lowest lean-mass share reported in GLP-1 trials. The muscle-loss calculator uses the same numbers. These are illustrative projections from published ranges, not a measurement of your body.

Honest limits

Every number here is an estimate. Predictive BMR equations carry error at the individual level, the 7,700 kcal rule overstates loss over months, and the muscle split is modeled from population ranges rather than a scan of you. A target scaled to total body weight is a starting point, not a prescription, and it can overestimate protein for people carrying more fat mass. Use this as a framework, and confirm the specifics with your clinician, especially the calorie floor while on a GLP-1.

Frequently asked

How many calories should I eat on a GLP-1?

There is no single right number. The calculator estimates your maintenance calories (TDEE) from your sex, age, height, weight and activity level, then subtracts the deficit that matches the pace you pick, using the classic estimate of 7,700 kcal per kilogram of body weight. Change the pace and the target moves. It is a planning estimate: confirm the calorie floor with your clinician while on a GLP-1.

How does this calculator estimate my calories?

It uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation for basal metabolic rate (BMR), the most validated predictive equation for healthy adults, then multiplies by a physical-activity factor to estimate total daily energy expenditure (TDEE). Your calorie target is TDEE minus the deficit implied by your chosen pace of weight loss.

How much protein does it recommend, and of what type?

It applies the 1.6 to 2.2 g/kg of body weight range that lean-mass research supports during weight loss, split across four meals. Hit it from complete, leucine-rich sources; collagen is low in leucine and does little for muscle despite the label.

What macros should I eat on a GLP-1?

Protein first: 1.6 g per kilogram of body weight a day is where the benefit to lean mass levels off, with 1.2 to 1.6 g/kg as the GLP-1 advisory range. Fat at 20 to 35 percent of calories is the adult range the National Academies give; the calculator uses 25 percent. Carbohydrate fills the rest of the calorie target. The calculator shows all three in grams for your numbers.

How much fiber should I eat on a GLP-1?

The Dietary Guidelines figure is 14 g of fiber per 1,000 calories, so about 21 to 25 g on a 1,500 to 1,800 calorie target. Fiber helps with constipation, one of the most common GLP-1 side effects. Raise it gradually and drink fluids with it, since a sudden jump can worsen bloating.

Does the calculator work for women?

Yes. Choose your sex first: the Mifflin-St Jeor equation uses a different constant for women (minus 161) than for men (plus 5), so the calorie target differs. The protein target uses the same 1.6 to 2.2 g/kg range for everyone, scaled to body weight, and it can overestimate protein for people carrying more fat mass.

How should I time meals when a GLP-1 has killed my appetite?

Spread your daily protein across three to five smaller meals rather than one or two large ones, because distributing protein through the day supports muscle better than loading it into a single meal. On a small appetite, eat protein first before other foods, front-load it earlier in the day when hunger is highest, lean on liquid sources like a shake or Greek yogurt on nausea days, and keep your largest meal away from the peak side-effect window after a dose. The calculator's meal planner shows the per-meal grams for the number of meals you choose.

Why does the chart show a line and a band?

Total weight loss is driven by the calorie deficit and is similar either way. What changes is the composition. Without enough protein and resistance training, a large share of the weight lost is lean mass (reviews put it at 15 to 40 percent of GLP-1 weight loss). With adequate protein and resistance training, much of that is spared: a meta-analysis of older adults who were not on a GLP-1 found resistance training offset roughly 93.5 percent of the lean mass that dieting alone would cost, so the best case is shown as a range, not a single point. The red line and green band make that difference visible.

Practical gear to hit your target

  • A shaker bottle makes a fast protein serving on the days appetite is low.
