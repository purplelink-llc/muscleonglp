GLP-1 macros: how to split protein, carbs and fat

A GLP-1 shrinks how much you eat, so the split of what you do eat matters more. Set protein first, then fat, then let carbohydrate fill the rest. Here is the order, the ranges behind each number, and a worked example.

Updated 2026-10-27 Evidence-based, every claim cited

Key takeaways

  • Protein comes first: about 1.2 to 1.6 g/kg a day in a four-society GLP-1 nutrition advisory, with the benefit to lean mass leveling off near 1.6 g/kg.1,2
  • Fat: 20 to 35 percent of calories, the adult range from the National Academies.3
  • Carbohydrate fills the remaining calories, ideally at least 130 g a day, the adult RDA.3
  • Fiber: about 14 g per 1,000 calories, which matters on a drug whose common side effects include constipation.4,5

Most macro advice is written for people who can eat as much as they like and need a reason to stop. On a GLP-1 the problem runs the other way: the medication cuts appetite, so total intake is small and every gram has to do work. A fixed percentage split (the familiar 40/30/30) can leave protein short when calories drop, because 30 percent of 1,400 calories is only 105 g. Setting protein in grams per kilogram first, and letting the other two fill around it, avoids that.

Step 1: protein, in grams, from your weight

A 2025 joint advisory from four nutrition and obesity societies puts protein at about 1.2 to 1.6 g per kilogram of body weight a day during GLP-1 weight loss.1 A meta-analysis of strength-training trials found the gain in lean mass from extra protein leveled off near 1.6 g/kg, so the top of that range is where most people stop seeing a benefit.2 The target is the total for the day. Split across three to five meals, it comes to roughly 25 to 40 g a meal for most adults.6

Step 2: fat, as a share of calories

The National Academies set the acceptable range for adult fat intake at 20 to 35 percent of calories.3 The low end leaves more room for protein and carbohydrate at a small calorie target. If rich, greasy meals sit badly on the medication, staying near the low end is a reasonable choice and costs nothing within that range.

Step 3: carbohydrate fills the rest

Whatever calories remain after protein and fat go to carbohydrate. The adult RDA for carbohydrate is 130 g a day, the amount that covers the brain's glucose needs.3 At a small calorie target with protein set high, carbohydrate can fall below the usual 45 to 65 percent range. That is expected, and keeping it at or above 130 g is a sensible floor. Carbohydrate also fuels the resistance training that keeps lean mass on, so a near-zero intake works against the goal of this site.

Step 4: fiber, scaled to calories

The Dietary Guidelines for Americans set fiber at 14 g per 1,000 calories.4 Constipation was among the most common side effects of semaglutide in the STEP 1 trial,5 and appetite loss tends to cut the bulky, fiber-rich foods first. Raise fiber gradually and drink fluids with it, since a sudden jump can worsen bloating.

A worked example

Take a 45-year-old woman, 5 ft 6 in and 210 lb, lightly active, aiming to lose about a pound a week. The calorie, protein and macro calculator puts her target near 1,725 calories. Protein at 1.6 g/kg is 152 g (35 percent of calories). Fat at 25 percent is 48 g. Carbohydrate takes the remaining 171 g (40 percent), above the 130 g floor. Fiber at 14 g per 1,000 calories is about 24 g.

What the evidence does not say

No trial has compared macro splits for muscle in people taking a GLP-1. The protein figures come from strength-training and weight-loss research in general adult populations, and the fat, carbohydrate and fiber ranges are general dietary reference values, not GLP-1 findings. In weight-loss research outside GLP-1s, total protein and resistance training matter for lean mass far more than the ratio of carbohydrate to fat, so a split you can actually eat on a small appetite beats a theoretically ideal one you cannot. People with kidney disease should ask a clinician before raising protein, and people with diabetes should set carbohydrate with their care team.

Frequently asked

What macros should I eat on a GLP-1?

Set protein first, at about 1.2 to 1.6 g per kilogram of body weight a day, the range a four-society GLP-1 nutrition advisory uses. Put fat at 20 to 35 percent of calories, the adult range from the National Academies. Carbohydrate fills the remaining calories, ideally with at least 130 g a day, the adult RDA. Aim for 14 g of fiber per 1,000 calories.

Is a low-carb or keto diet better for muscle on a GLP-1?

No trial has compared macro splits for muscle in people on a GLP-1. In general weight-loss research, total protein and resistance training matter for lean mass far more than the carbohydrate-to-fat ratio. A split you can keep eating on a small appetite is the one that works.

Why does fiber matter more on a GLP-1?

Constipation was among the most common side effects in the semaglutide trials, and appetite suppression tends to cut fiber-rich foods first. Fiber at about 14 g per 1,000 calories, raised gradually and taken with fluids, is the usual dietary target.
