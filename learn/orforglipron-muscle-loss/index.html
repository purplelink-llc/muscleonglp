Orforglipron (Foundayo) and muscle loss: what the trial data shows

Orforglipron is the GLP-1 pill the FDA approved in April 2026. Its main trial reported weight, not muscle. Here is what is known, what is missing, and what protects muscle on it anyway.

Updated October 27, 2026 Evidence-based, every claim cited

Key takeaways

  • The FDA approved orforglipron as Foundayo on April 1, 2026, for chronic weight management. It is a once-daily pill with no food or water restrictions.2,10
  • In the ATTAIN-1 trial, the 36 mg dose produced 11.2 percent average weight loss over 72 weeks, against 2.1 percent on placebo, counting everyone assigned a dose.1
  • ATTAIN-1 reported no body-composition (DXA) data, and we found no published orforglipron lean-mass study as of October 2026. The muscle share of its weight loss is unknown.
  • In trials of injectable GLP-1s, lean mass has made up roughly 15 to 40 percent or more of the weight lost.3 There is no reason yet to expect a pill to differ.
  • Resistance training and enough protein protect muscle regardless of which GLP-1 caused the deficit.5,6

Orforglipron is a GLP-1 receptor agonist, the same drug class as semaglutide (Ozempic, Wegovy), but it is a small molecule rather than a peptide (a short protein chain). That is what lets it work as a pill taken at any time of day, without the fasting rules that oral semaglutide carries. It acts on one receptor, GLP-1, where tirzepatide (Mounjaro, Zepbound) acts on two.

For someone who wants to keep muscle, the question is the same as for any GLP-1: how much of the weight that comes off is fat, and how much is lean tissue. For orforglipron that question has no direct answer yet. This article sets out what the trial measured, what it did not, and what to do in the meantime.

How much weight does orforglipron produce?

ATTAIN-1 randomized 3,127 adults with obesity and without diabetes to orforglipron 6 mg, 12 mg or 36 mg once daily, or placebo, for 72 weeks. Counting everyone assigned a dose, average weight change was −7.5 percent on 6 mg, −8.4 percent on 12 mg and −11.2 percent on 36 mg, against −2.1 percent on placebo. On 36 mg, 54.6 percent of people lost at least 10 percent of their weight and 18.4 percent lost at least 20 percent.1 Among people who stayed on the highest dose, the average was 12.4 percent.2

For comparison, semaglutide 2.4 mg averaged 14.9 percent over 68 weeks in STEP 1, and tirzepatide 15 mg averaged 20.9 percent over 72 weeks in SURMOUNT-1.7,8 These are separate trials with different populations, not a head-to-head comparison, so the gap is a rough guide rather than a measured difference.

Side effects were mostly gastrointestinal and mild to moderate. Adverse events led 5.3 to 10.3 percent of people on orforglipron to stop, against 2.7 percent on placebo.1

What the body-composition data shows

None has been published. The ATTAIN-1 abstract reports weight, waist circumference, blood pressure and blood lipids, but not fat mass or lean mass.1 A search of PubMed in October 2026 turned up no orforglipron study measuring body composition with DXA (a low-dose X-ray scan that separates fat, lean tissue and bone).

The injectable GLP-1s give the closest guide. A review of the trial data put lean mass at anywhere from about 15 percent to 40 percent or more of the weight lost, depending on the drug, the population and how it was measured.3 In the tirzepatide SURMOUNT-1 DXA substudy, about a quarter of the weight lost was lean mass.4 Orforglipron works through the same receptor as semaglutide, so a similar range is a reasonable working assumption. It is still an assumption until someone scans people taking it.

A smaller loss still puts muscle at stake

A smaller average loss means less lean tissue lost in absolute terms, if the share is similar. On a 220 lb starting weight, 11.2 percent is about 25 lb. If a quarter to a third of that were lean mass, as in the injectable trials, that is roughly 6 to 8 lb. The muscle loss calculator runs this for your own numbers. Lean mass is not all muscle (it includes water and organ tissue), but it is the best measure the trials report.

The appetite effect matters as much as the size of the loss. A GLP-1 pill suppresses hunger the same way an injection does, and protein is the nutrient people most often fall short on when they eat less.

What still protects your muscle, regardless of the drug

The two levers with the strongest evidence do not depend on the medication. In a meta-analysis of older adults losing weight through caloric restriction, resistance training offset about 93.5 percent of the lean-mass loss that dieting alone produced.5 A dose-response meta-analysis found that protein up to about 1.6 grams per kilogram of body weight a day kept improving resistance-training gains.6 A 2025 joint advisory on nutrition during GLP-1 treatment recommends about 1.2 to 1.6 g/kg a day.9 These findings come from broader weight-loss and training studies rather than orforglipron trials, but they are the best evidence available.

What the evidence does not say

The weight figures here come from the ATTAIN-1 abstract and from reporting on the FDA approval. No body-composition result for orforglipron has been published, so every statement about its fat-to-lean ratio is an extrapolation from injectable GLP-1s, not a finding. ATTAIN-1 enrolled adults without diabetes, so its numbers may not carry over to people with type 2 diabetes. This article gives no dosing information; the doses named are the trial doses, and dosing decisions belong with your prescriber.

The bottom line

Orforglipron brings GLP-1 weight loss to a pill, with an average loss in its main trial smaller than tirzepatide's and somewhat smaller than semaglutide's. Whether its muscle cost is proportionally the same is unknown until a DXA study is published. Until then, the practical answer is the same as for any GLP-1: train against resistance, eat enough protein, and track strength rather than the scale alone.

Frequently asked

Does orforglipron cause muscle loss?

Probably some, as with every GLP-1 medication, but nobody has measured it yet. The ATTAIN-1 trial reported weight, waist and blood markers, not body composition, and no orforglipron DXA study had been published as of October 2026. In trials of injectable GLP-1s, lean mass has made up anywhere from about 15 percent to 40 percent or more of the weight lost.

How much weight do people lose on orforglipron?

In ATTAIN-1, adults with obesity and without diabetes lost an average of 11.2 percent of body weight over 72 weeks on 36 mg, 8.4 percent on 12 mg and 7.5 percent on 6 mg, against 2.1 percent on placebo, counting everyone who was assigned a dose. Among those who stayed on the highest dose, the average was 12.4 percent.

Is orforglipron FDA approved?

Yes. The FDA approved orforglipron for chronic weight management on April 1, 2026, under the brand name Foundayo, for adults with obesity, or overweight with at least one weight-related condition. It is a once-daily pill that can be taken without food or water restrictions.
