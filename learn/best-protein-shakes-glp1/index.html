Best protein shakes for GLP-1 users: a 5-point label checklist

On a GLP-1, a protein shake often carries a whole meal's worth of muscle protection. Here is how to read the label, which format fits your stomach, and what to skip.

Updated October 27, 2026 Evidence-based, every claim cited

Key takeaways

  • Look for 25 to 30 grams of protein per serving, which supplies about 2.5 to 3 grams of leucine, the per-meal amount that appears to maximize the muscle-building response.1
  • Choose a complete protein: whey, milk, soy, or a pea and rice blend. Collagen does not count toward muscle.
  • On nausea days, pick the format you can finish. 43.9 percent of people on semaglutide 2.4 mg reported nausea in the STEP 1 to 3 trials.2
  • Prefer third-party tested products. The FDA does not approve supplements for safety or effectiveness before they are sold.3
  • A shake supports a daily target of about 1.2 to 1.6 g/kg of body weight.4 It protects muscle only alongside resistance training.

Short answer. The best protein shake on a GLP-1 is one you can finish that delivers 25 to 30 grams of complete protein.1 Whey isolate meets that in one scoop. Clear whey and small ready-to-drink shakes are easier on nausea days. A third-party seal is your best evidence that the label matches the tub.

The reason a shake matters more on a GLP-1 is arithmetic. Reviews of the trial data put lean mass (everything that is not fat, mostly muscle) at roughly 15 to 40 percent of the weight lost on these drugs.5 Protein is one of the two levers with evidence for shrinking that share. However, a suppressed appetite makes protein the hardest nutrient to eat. A shake solves that by putting a full meal's protein into a few hundred milliliters.

The 5-point label checklist

1. Protein per serving: 25 to 30 grams. That amount of high-quality protein supplies about 2.5 to 3 grams of leucine (the amino acid that switches on muscle protein synthesis). That is the per-meal dose that appears to maximize the muscle-building response in most adults.1 Adults over about 60 need closer to 30 grams for the same response. A shake with 10 or 15 grams is a snack, not a protein meal.

2. A complete protein source. Whey, casein, milk protein and soy are complete on their own. Pea protein is low in methionine and rice protein is low in lysine, so a pea and rice blend covers what each lacks. Collagen is the exception to avoid as your main protein. It is low in leucine and lacks the essential amino acid tryptophan, so it does little for muscle however many grams the label shows.7

3. A format you can actually finish. Nausea is common on these drugs: in a pooled analysis of the STEP 1 to 3 trials, 43.9 percent of people on semaglutide 2.4 mg reported it, versus 16.1 percent on placebo.2 Symptoms clustered around dose increases. On those days, a thick creamy shake you leave half-full delivers half the protein. Thin, cold and small formats tend to go down more easily.

4. Third-party testing. The FDA does not review dietary supplements for safety or effectiveness before they reach the shelf.3 Independent certification programs (e.g., NSF Certified for Sport, Informed Choice) test products against their labels and screen for contaminants.8 A certification seal is the closest thing to proof that the 25 grams on the label is in the scoop.

5. Ingredients that work against you. If your stomach is already unsettled, read the ingredient list for sugar alcohols and added fiber, which some people find bloating. Skip "proprietary blends" that hide how much of each protein is inside. If dairy bothers you, whey isolate is processed to remove most of the lactose that whey concentrate keeps.

Which format fits your situation

The same 25 to 30 grams can arrive in several formats. Match the format to your appetite on that day, not to the marketing.

What a shake cannot do

Protein supplies the raw material. Resistance training is the signal that tells your body to keep muscle while you lose weight, and protein without training does far less.6 Plan the shake for the meals your appetite will skip, and pair the week with two or three strength sessions. The best exercises to preserve muscle covers that half.

A shake also does not set your daily target. The joint advisory from four medical societies suggests 1.2 to 1.6 g/kg a day during active GLP-1 weight loss.4 The calorie and protein calculator turns that into grams, and the protein timing guide shows how to split it across a small appetite.

Frequently asked

What is the best protein shake for someone on a GLP-1?

The best shake is one you can finish that delivers about 25 to 30 grams of complete protein, which supplies roughly 2.5 to 3 grams of leucine. Whey isolate meets that in one scoop. On nausea days, a clear whey or a small ready-to-drink shake is often easier to get down. Prefer a product with third-party testing.

Are plant-based protein shakes good enough on Ozempic?

Yes, if the shake is complete. Soy is complete on its own, and a pea and rice blend covers what each protein lacks. Plant proteins usually carry less leucine per gram than whey, so aim for the higher end of 25 to 30 grams. The vegetarian and vegan guide covers the details.

What protein shake is easiest to drink with GLP-1 nausea?

Thin, cold, low-volume options tend to go down more easily than thick creamy shakes. Clear whey isolate drinks like juice, and a small ready-to-drink shake needs no mixing. The nausea-day meal guide has food options too.

How much protein do I need a day on a GLP-1?

A joint advisory from four medical societies suggests 1.2 to 1.6 g/kg of body weight a day during active weight loss. The benefit of protein for fat-free mass leveled off near 1.6 g/kg in a large meta-analysis.6
