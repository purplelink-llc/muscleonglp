Muscle strength check

Two tests you can do at home, compared with the cut-offs clinicians use for low muscle strength. Run it every four weeks on a GLP-1 and watch the trend, not the scale.

Short answer. The European sarcopenia consensus flags low muscle strength at a grip below 27 kg in men or 16 kg in women, or a five-times chair stand slower than 15 seconds.1 Strength is worth tracking on a GLP-1 because a meaningful share of the weight lost can be lean mass.3

Grip strength

Five chair stands cut-off 15 seconds; faster is better

How to do the two tests

Grip strength. Use a hand dynamometer. Stand, arm at your side with the elbow bent at a right angle, and squeeze as hard as you can for a few seconds. Do three squeezes with your stronger hand and record the best. Use the same hand and the same device every time, because readings differ between devices.

Five-times chair stand. Sit in a standard chair with your back against it and your arms crossed over your chest. Time five full stands, sitting back down each time, as fast as you safely can. The test needs no equipment and gives highly consistent results from one session to the next.2

What the cut-offs mean

The European Working Group on Sarcopenia in Older People (EWGSOP2) leads with low muscle strength when it screens for sarcopenia, and grip strength or the chair stand is how it measures that.1 The cut-offs were set for older adults. A younger person can sit well above them and still be losing strength, which is why the change since your last reading is often the more useful number. On a GLP-1, resistance training and enough protein are the two levers with evidence for holding onto muscle.3,4

Frequently asked

What is a normal grip strength?

The European sarcopenia consensus (EWGSOP2) treats grip strength below 27 kg in men or 16 kg in women as low muscle strength, one of its main warning signs. Above those cut-offs is not low, but grip varies with age and body size, so your own trend over time tells you more than one reading.

How do you do the chair-stand test?

Sit in a standard chair with your arms crossed over your chest. Time how long it takes to stand up fully and sit back down five times, as fast as you safely can. More than 15 seconds is the EWGSOP2 cut-off for low strength.

How often should I check grip strength on a GLP-1?

Every four weeks is enough to see a trend without chasing day-to-day noise. Use the same hand, the same device and the same time of day, and record the best of three squeezes.
