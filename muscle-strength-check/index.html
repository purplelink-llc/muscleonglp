Muscle strength check

Two tests you can do at home, compared with the cut-offs clinicians use for low muscle strength. Run it every four weeks on a GLP-1 and watch the trend, not the scale.

Short answer. The European sarcopenia consensus flags low muscle strength at a grip below 27 kg in men or 16 kg in women, or a five-times chair stand slower than 15 seconds.1 Strength is worth tracking on a GLP-1 because a meaningful share of the weight lost can be lean mass.2

Grip strength

Five chair stands cut-off 15 seconds; faster is better

Keep the numbers somewhere. A trend needs a record. Note today's grip and chair-stand time with the date, or use the Muscle-on-GLP-1 Tracker ($9, printable): its weekly strength check works with any movement you retest, and these two are the easiest.

How to do the two tests

Grip strength. Use a hand dynamometer. Stand, arm at your side with the elbow bent at a right angle, and squeeze as hard as you can for a few seconds. Do three squeezes with your stronger hand and record the best. Use the same hand and the same device every time, because readings differ between devices.

Five-times chair stand. Sit in a standard chair with your back against it and your arms crossed over your chest. Time five full stands, sitting back down each time, as fast as you safely can. The test needs no equipment and gives highly consistent results from one session to the next.3

Grip strength by age

Grip peaks in the thirties and falls with each decade after that. The figures below come from 60,803 grip measurements in twelve British studies.4 The median is the typical value for that age, and the 10th centile is the reading that only one adult in ten of that age falls below.

The chair stand has age references too, though only from 60 up. A meta-analysis of 13 papers on older adults put the average time for five stands at 11.4 seconds for ages 60 to 69, 12.6 seconds for 70 to 79, and 14.8 seconds for 80 to 89. Slower than that is worse than average for the age group.5

What the cut-offs mean

The European Working Group on Sarcopenia in Older People (EWGSOP2) leads with low muscle strength when it screens for sarcopenia, and grip strength or the chair stand is how it measures that.1 The cut-offs were set for older adults. A younger person can sit well above them and still be losing strength, which is why the change since your last reading is often the more useful number. On a GLP-1, resistance training and enough protein are the two levers with evidence for holding onto muscle.2,6

What happens to grip strength on semaglutide

On average, not much. A 2026 meta-analysis pooled ten studies with 660 people on semaglutide and found a grip change of −0.16 kg, too small to tell apart from no change (95% confidence interval −1.98 to 1.65 kg).7 However, the studies disagreed widely: the range expected for a new study ran from about 6 kg lost to 6 kg gained. The authors rated the evidence very low certainty, mostly because the studies had no comparison group. An average near zero therefore says little about you. Retesting every four weeks shows which side of that range you are on.

A 2026 study in Cell Reports Medicine points the same way. In obese mice, GLP-1 medicines lowered absolute muscle mass and strength but raised strength relative to body weight, and the mice ran better. In a small proof-of-concept trial, patients with obesity improved their body composition without losing strength.8 Strength relative to body weight can rise while the absolute number falls, so watch both.

Frequently asked

What is a normal grip strength?

The European sarcopenia consensus (EWGSOP2) treats grip strength below 27 kg in men or 16 kg in women as low muscle strength, one of its main warning signs. Typical grip also depends on age: in a study of 60,803 measurements, median grip peaked at about 51 kg in men and 31 kg in women in their thirties, then fell to about 39 kg and 24 kg at age 70. Your own trend over time tells you more than one reading.

How do you do the chair-stand test?

Sit in a standard chair with your arms crossed over your chest. Time how long it takes to stand up fully and sit back down five times, as fast as you safely can. More than 15 seconds is the EWGSOP2 cut-off for low strength.

What is a normal chair-stand time for my age?

For adults 60 and over, a meta-analysis of 13 papers put the average time for five chair stands at 11.4 seconds for ages 60 to 69, 12.6 seconds for 70 to 79, and 14.8 seconds for 80 to 89. Slower than that is worse than average for the age group. More than 15 seconds at any age is the EWGSOP2 cut-off for low strength.

How often should I check grip strength on a GLP-1?

Every four weeks is enough to see a trend without chasing day-to-day noise. Use the same hand, the same device and the same time of day, and record the best of three squeezes.
