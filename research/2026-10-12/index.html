GLP-1 and muscle research: October 6-12, 2026

This week's papers span the full spectrum from mouse-model experiments to clinical narrative reviews, with several directly addressing the central question of lean mass preservation during GLP-1-based weight loss. A recurring theme is that fat-free mass loss on these medications is not synonymous with skeletal muscle loss, but that susceptible populations—older adults, those with limited muscle reserves—warrant particular attention. Emerging combination strategies (resistance training, protein intake, investigational anabolic agents) continue to be evaluated against a backdrop of still-limited long-term human data.

More than just adipose. The impact of GLP1Ra on lean tissue.

American journal of physiology. Cell physiology · 2026-10-06 · Julien M, Jeromson S, Wright DC

This review, published in a physiology journal, focuses specifically on the effects of GLP-1 receptor agonists on lean tissue—skeletal muscle and bone—beyond their well-known impact on fat mass. It discusses key findings showing that GLP-1RA treatment results in reductions in lean tissue, reviews approaches that have been implemented to mitigate these changes, and identifies important areas for future research. The paper is a narrative review and does not present new primary data.

Why it matters: This is one of the few reviews dedicated entirely to GLP-1RA effects on skeletal muscle and bone, directly addressing the core concern of lean mass loss during GLP-1-based obesity treatment.

What this means for you: This is one more reason to monitor lean mass and bone health proactively when using GLP-1 receptor agonists for weight loss.

Weight Loss or Muscle Loss? Cellular Mechanisms and Clinical Strategies for Preserving Lean Mass During GLP-1-Based Therapy.

Diabetes, obesity & metabolism · 2026-10-07 · Sukurica E, Sengul GF, Uysal Cinar F

This review examines cellular and tissue-level mechanisms underlying lean mass changes during GLP-1-based therapy and evaluates strategies for preserving skeletal muscle. It cautions that reductions in fat-free or lean body mass should not be equated with loss of contractile skeletal muscle, as these compartments also include water, organs, and connective tissue. Preclinical studies and limited human evidence suggest possible improvements in skeletal muscle quality through enhanced insulin sensitivity, reduced myosteatosis, and favorable mitochondrial adaptations, though the proportion of lean mass reduction attributable to true muscle loss and its functional significance remain uncertain. The review identifies individualized protein intake, progressive resistance exercise, management of gastrointestinal side effects, and functional monitoring as reasonable preservation strategies.

Why it matters: This review provides the most comprehensive mechanistic and clinical synthesis available on the distinction between lean mass loss and true skeletal muscle loss during GLP-1 therapy, directly informing how outcomes should be measured and interpreted.

What this means for you: This is one more reason to monitor strength and physical performance—not just scale weight or body composition scans—when tracking outcomes during GLP-1-based weight loss.

Exercise, Nutrition and GLP-1 Medicines to Improve Body Weight and Muscle Function in Heart Failure with Preserved Ejection Fraction.

Current heart failure reports · 2026-10-08 · Prokopidis K et al.

This narrative review examines interventions targeting muscle strength, body composition, and functional capacity in heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF) complicated by sarcopenia and/or obesity. It finds that concurrent endurance and resistance training improves aerobic capacity and quality of life, while protein supplementation may preserve muscle mass and caloric restriction may reduce it. GLP-1 medicines are noted to produce greater weight loss and performance benefits than SGLT2 inhibitors, and the review suggests that combining resistance exercise with adequate protein intake may help preserve muscle during weight loss in this population.

Why it matters: The review synthesizes evidence that resistance training and protein intake can counteract muscle loss during GLP-1-driven weight loss, even in a high-risk cardiac population with sarcopenia.

What this means for you: This adds to the case for pairing resistance exercise with adequate protein intake when using GLP-1 medications for weight loss, particularly in populations at elevated risk of muscle loss.

GLP-1 receptor agonists in perimenopause and early postmenopause: clinical role, cardiometabolic benefits and knowledge gaps.

Climacteric : the journal of the International Menopause Society · 2026-10-08 · Ozcivit Erkan IB et al.

This narrative review examines the use of GLP-1 receptor agonists in perimenopausal and early postmenopausal women, a population experiencing adverse shifts in body composition, insulin sensitivity, and cardiometabolic health. Beyond established effects on weight and glucose, the review notes that GLP-1 RAs have demonstrated favorable effects on visceral adiposity and highlights attention to bone and muscle health as areas of clinical interest. However, the authors emphasize that menopause-specific evidence remains limited and that important knowledge gaps persist regarding long-term safety and effects on muscle and bone in this population.

Why it matters: Perimenopause is associated with accelerated loss of muscle and bone mass, and this review flags that GLP-1 RA effects on these tissues in midlife women remain poorly characterized—a gap directly relevant to body composition outcomes.

What this means for you: Worth discussing with a clinician, particularly for perimenopausal or postmenopausal women using GLP-1 medications, given the acknowledged gaps in evidence on muscle and bone outcomes in this group.

An RNA thermogenic therapy to preserve lean mass and enhance metabolic health during GLP-1 weight loss.

Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America · 2026-09-25 · Thorne AK et al.

This mouse study developed adipose-directed antisense oligonucleotides (ASOs) targeting ZFP423, a transcriptional repressor of brown and beige adipocyte identity, as a strategy to activate adipose thermogenesis. In diet-induced obese mice, ZFP423 ASO treatment increased energy expenditure and improved metabolic parameters. When combined with the GLP-1 receptor agonist semaglutide, the combination produced greater weight and fat loss than either treatment alone while preserving lean mass. All findings are preclinical; no human data are presented.

Why it matters: This mouse study demonstrates proof-of-concept that targeting adipose thermogenesis alongside GLP-1RA treatment can enhance fat loss while preserving lean mass, pointing toward a potential future strategy for improving body composition quality during GLP-1-based therapy.
