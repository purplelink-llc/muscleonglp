Weight, Fat Mass, and Lean Mass Changes After Bariatric Surgery and GLP-1-Based Therapy: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.

Obesity surgery · 2026-11-13 · Urbina J et al.

This systematic review and meta-analysis of 31 studies (5,616 participants) compared body composition changes after metabolic/bariatric surgery versus GLP-1-based therapy at 12 months. Bariatric surgery was associated with greater total weight loss and absolute fat-mass loss than GLP-1-based therapy; greater absolute lean-mass loss accompanied the larger weight reduction with surgery, but the analysis did not establish poorer proportional lean-mass preservation for either approach. The authors note substantial heterogeneity and call for standardized comparative studies incorporating muscle strength and physical function.

Why it matters: Directly quantifies absolute and proportional lean-mass loss with GLP-1-based therapy versus surgery, highlighting that absolute lean-mass loss is a real concern and that strength and function data are currently missing from the evidence base.

What this means for you: This is one more reason to track strength and lean mass — not just body weight — throughout GLP-1-based weight-loss treatment.

Trevogrumab (anti-GDF8) with or without garetosmab (anti-activin A) for prevention of muscle loss during semaglutide-induced weight reduction in people with obesity (COURAGE): a two-part, phase 2, double-blind, placebo-controlled, randomised trial.

Lancet (London, England) · 2026-11-11 · Mosenzon O et al.

The COURAGE phase 2 RCT (975 participants with obesity, BMI ≥30) tested antibodies blocking myostatin (trevogrumab) and activin A (garetosmab) added to semaglutide 2.4 mg weekly for 26 weeks (Part A) or 52 weeks (Part B). In Part A, the semaglutide-plus-placebo group lost 6.6% of lean mass; trevogrumab at both doses attenuated this loss, with an estimated treatment difference of +3.1% (95% CI 1.9 to [truncated]) versus placebo. The combination of trevogrumab and garetosmab was also evaluated. This is a phase 2 trial; full efficacy and safety data are pending.

Why it matters: This is the first large phase 2 RCT to quantify semaglutide-induced lean-mass loss and demonstrate that pharmacological blockade of negative muscle regulators can meaningfully attenuate it.

What this means for you: Worth discussing with a prescribing clinician as evidence on muscle-preserving adjuncts to GLP-1 therapy continues to emerge.

Resistance exercise to attenuate lean mass loss during tirzepatide therapy for obesity: a randomized controlled trial.

Nature medicine · 2026-11-09 · Green DJ et al.

This two-arm RCT (120 adults with overweight or obesity, 40 weeks) randomized participants to tirzepatide alone or tirzepatide plus thrice-weekly resistance exercise training. Both groups achieved similar total weight loss (~22 kg). Resistance exercise did not significantly alter fat-mass loss but significantly reduced loss of total lean soft tissue mass (tirzepatide alone: −4.67 kg; tirzepatide plus resistance exercise: −3.57 kg; between-group difference 1.09 kg, 95% CI 0.43–1.75 kg). Upper and lower body muscle strength decreased with tirzepatide alone but increased with the addition of resistance exercise across all three measured exercises.

Why it matters: This RCT provides direct, quantified evidence that resistance exercise training preserves lean mass and increases muscle strength during tirzepatide-induced weight loss, where tirzepatide alone reduced strength.

What this means for you: This is one more reason to incorporate regular resistance training alongside tirzepatide or similar GLP-1-based therapy.

Aging exacerbates muscle loss induced by semaglutide treatment in female mice Preprint

Preprint · 2026-11-13 · Rout S et al.

This preprint mouse study treated 21-month-old obese C57BL/6J mice with semaglutide for 3 weeks and assessed skeletal muscle mass and strength. Semaglutide induced dose-dependent weight loss in both sexes. Lean-mass loss was substantially greater in females (8.6–8.3%) than males (2.3–6.6%), and female mice showed up to 14.2% reduction in quadriceps mass at the higher dose. Muscle force-generating capacity was not compromised in females but was reduced by 19.2% in males at the higher dose. The authors note this contrasts with findings in young mice and highlight age as a potentially important modifier. As a preprint animal study, findings require confirmation in humans.

Why it matters: Provides preclinical evidence that aging may amplify semaglutide-induced skeletal muscle loss and functional impairment, with notable sex differences, raising questions about risk in older adults on GLP-1 therapy.

What this means for you: This is one more reason to monitor lean mass and muscle function in older individuals using GLP-1 receptor agonists, and to discuss age-related risk with a prescribing clinician.

Structured nutritional management of insulin resistance in women with PMOS (formerly PCOS) receiving GLP-1RA therapy.

Diabetes research and clinical practice · 2026-11-13 · Joshi S, Pawar A

This structured review examined GLP-1 receptor agonist use in women with Polyendocrine Metabolic Ovarian Syndrome (PMOS, formerly PCOS). GLP-1RAs produced consistent reductions in weight, androgens, and fasting insulin, but insulin-resistance outcomes were heterogeneous and reversed after treatment withdrawal. The authors identify appetite suppression as a driver of inadequate protein intake and micronutrient depletion, and propose a three-phase clinical nutrition protocol emphasizing protein optimization, micronutrient repletion, and resistance exercise alongside GLP-1RA therapy.

Why it matters: The review explicitly links GLP-1RA-induced appetite suppression to inadequate protein intake and lean-mass risk in a specific female population, and proposes structured nutritional and exercise countermeasures.

What this means for you: This adds to the case for prioritizing daily protein targets when appetite is suppressed by GLP-1 therapy, particularly in women with underlying metabolic conditions.

Nutrition Care Strategies at the Intersection of Menopause and Obesity Pharmacotherapy.

The Journal of nutrition · 2026-11-10 · Christensen S et al.

This narrative review examines nutritional risks for perimenopausal women using incretin mimetic pharmacotherapies. It identifies overlapping vulnerabilities: estrogen deficiency combined with GLP-1-driven appetite suppression, reduced energy intake, and gastrointestinal side effects that together heighten risk for sarcopenic obesity and osteoporosis. Clinical strategies discussed include protein optimization, micronutrient monitoring, menopausal hormone therapy for musculoskeletal health, and multidisciplinary care.

Why it matters: Identifies perimenopausal women on GLP-1 therapy as a group with compounded risk for lean-mass and bone loss, and frames protein and micronutrient adequacy as central mitigation strategies.

What this means for you: This adds to the case for monitoring lean mass and nutritional status closely in perimenopausal women using GLP-1-based therapies.

Nutritional Strategies for Managing Gastrointestinal Adverse Effects Associated with GLP-1 Receptor Agonist and Dual GIP/GLP-1 Receptor Agonist Therapy: A Narrative Review with Practical Recommendations Preprint

Preprint · 2026-11-13 · Baldassari G et al.

This preprint narrative review examines gastrointestinal adverse effects of GLP-1 and dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonists — including nausea, vomiting, constipation, and bloating — and their impact on dietary quality and nutritional adequacy. The authors present symptom-specific dietary recommendations covering meal distribution, fat and fiber intake, hydration, and food choices. A key theme is that GI side effects can compromise protein intake, increase micronutrient deficiency risk, and threaten muscle mass preservation during treatment.

Why it matters: Directly links GLP-1-induced gastrointestinal side effects to reduced protein intake and muscle-mass risk, and proposes practical nutritional strategies to mitigate these effects.

What this means for you: This adds to the case for prioritizing adequate daily protein intake even when gastrointestinal symptoms reduce appetite and food tolerance during GLP-1 therapy.
