How this is estimated (and the research behind it)

Lean mass, not only muscle. The research measures lean mass: everything that is not fat. That is mostly muscle, but it also includes water and organs. This page says lean mass when it means the measured figure and muscle when it means the everyday idea.

The baseline share. Reviews of the trial data put lean mass at anywhere from about 15 percent to 40 to 60 percent of the weight lost on a GLP-1 medication.1 Within that spread, the medication matters. The estimator uses a baseline of about 33 percent for semaglutide and other GLP-1s, and about 26 percent for tirzepatide, the value measured in the DXA substudy of the SURMOUNT-1 trial.1,4

Why two scenarios. The share of loss that is muscle is not fixed. Without enough protein and without resistance training, the estimate sits at the model's high end, 40 percent for semaglutide. Some studies report more, up to 60 percent.1 With both in place, it moves toward the low end. The size of that shift comes from a meta-analysis of older adults with obesity on caloric restriction, not GLP-1 users, where resistance training offset about 93.5 percent of the lean mass that dieting alone would cost.2 Applying 93.5 percent to a GLP-1 baseline gives about 2 percent, which is an extrapolation, so the best case is shown as a range: that 2 percent floor up to 15 percent, the low end of the lean-mass shares reported in GLP-1 trials.1 No trial has measured the 2 percent figure in people taking a GLP-1.

Your current-habits estimate. The headline range scales between those scenarios using your reported protein habit and resistance training. The two calculators on this site use the same shared numbers for the no-plan case. Protein and training both count, with training weighted as the primary driver because the offset in the research is a training effect. Protein enables it: benefit to fat-free mass leveled off near 1.6 g/kg of body weight, which is the target used here for the high-protein setting.3

Lean mass at risk. If you enter a goal weight, the tool multiplies each scenario's lean-mass share by the total weight you plan to lose, giving an approximate range of lean mass at stake. This is a projection from population ranges, not a prediction of your specific outcome.

Honest limits

Every number here is an estimate assembled from group averages. The published range, from about 15 percent to 40 to 60 percent, is wide because trials differ in population, dose, and how body composition was measured, and DXA itself carries error. The 93.5 percent offset is a single meta-analytic figure from people who were not taking a GLP-1, and it will not hold identically for every person or program. Your genetics, training quality, sleep, and starting body composition all move the real result. Treat this as a framework for a conversation with your clinician, not a measurement, a prediction, or medical advice.

Frequently asked

How much of GLP-1 weight loss is muscle?

Reviews of the trial data put lean mass at anywhere from about 15 percent to 40 to 60 percent of the weight lost on a GLP-1 medication (Neeland 2024). The DXA substudy of the SURMOUNT-1 tirzepatide trial measured lean mass at about 26 percent of total loss. The exact share depends on the medication, your protein intake, and whether you do resistance training.

How does this estimator work?

It starts from a published baseline share of lean mass for your medication (about 33 percent for semaglutide and other GLP-1s, about 26 percent for tirzepatide from the SURMOUNT-1 DXA substudy), then shifts the estimate toward the low or high end of the published range based on your protein habit and resistance training. The shift uses the finding that resistance training offset about 93.5 percent of the lean mass that dieting alone would cost (Sardeli 2018). It is an illustrative projection from population ranges, not a measurement of your body.

Can I lower how much muscle I lose?

The evidence points to two levers. Eat enough protein, around 1.6 g/kg of body weight or more, which is where benefit to fat-free mass leveled off in the research (Morton 2018). And do resistance training, which in a meta-analysis offset about 93.5 percent of the lean mass that caloric restriction alone would cost (Sardeli 2018). Together they move the estimate toward the low end of the range.
